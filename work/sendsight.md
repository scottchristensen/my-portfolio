## Background

*(Interactive demo: [SendSight interactive demo](https://app.sendsight.ai/demo))*

### Where it started

I came up with SendSight while leading growth design at [Mercury](https://mercury.com). I was mapping our in-product and lifecycle emails so they'd feel like one experience, and I hit the same wall I'd hit at Expedia and everywhere else: nobody had a clear picture of which emails were going out, to whom, and why. The answer lived in an ESP only a few marketers could log into.

I believe lifecycle email is part of the product. It's often the first and last thing a user sees from you, yet product teams rarely know what's being sent. The usual workaround is to screenshot every email into Figma and stitch the journeys together by hand, which leaves you with a map that's out of date the day it's finished.

### Taking the leap

I kept hearing the same pain in growth and lifecycle Slack communities, but nobody was solving it. One company had tried: Plot was venture-funded in 2020 and shut down in 2024. That told me the problem was real. It also made me doubt this was a venture-scale business, so we chose to bootstrap.

The final push came in November 2025. Working with Opus 4.5, I saw how quickly a small team could now build production software. I called Seth, a longtime friend who had been building production apps for clients at his AI consultancy. We were friends long before we were business partners, and our skills complement each other. I left to build SendSight full time.

### Figuring out who it's for

Before we wrote any production code, I designed a prototype of the vision and took it to customers. We talked with 100+ lifecycle marketers, growth leaders, and agencies in about three months. The pain ran deep enough that people would pay: our first paying pilots signed on before the real product existed, and we built the production version alongside them.

We came to lifecycle marketing as outsiders, and it took nine or ten months of calls and conferences before we felt we had our heads above water. We had planned a self-serve motion for small teams, but the pain hits hardest on large teams, where many people need visibility. That meant founder-led sales and enterprise procurement.

SendSight was also almost too abstract to explain. People couldn't picture what we were selling, and illustrated animations didn't help. So we put the real UI on the website with some parts locked down, which is how the demo above came about. It finally made clear what SendSight does.

### Designing the MCP

We built SendSight for lifecycle marketers. Then it started spreading inside customer organizations, because product managers and designers wanted to see what their users were receiving. So we leaned in, and I designed an MCP that lets anyone ask Claude about live journeys in plain language, without an ESP seat. There are no screens to design in an MCP. The design material is tool names, descriptions, and response shapes. The breakthrough was getting live journeys to render inline in the chat.

Wider access worried lifecycle teams, who didn't want their data misread, so I designed guardrails into it: a Viewer role, metrics labeled as lifetime snapshots, every claim linked back to its source, and drafts that stay private and never touch the ESP.

It's still early, and enterprise sales cycles run four to six months or more. At least a dozen times this year I thought we'd hit a dead end. Each time, we took the next step and found just enough validation to keep going.
